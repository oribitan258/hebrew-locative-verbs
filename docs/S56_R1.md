# S56-v1 release R1: what it contains and how it works

**Status: RELEASED 2026-11-20 as tag `s56-r1`, a PROVISIONAL infrastructure milestone** (Ori). It is **not** a
validated, final identification system.
- It was built and corrected the same day, before any annotation (§0).
- The release conditions and the known limits of identification are in §0.1.
- Decision **D-057** (§8, accepted).
- The gold samples are **frozen** (§3).
- Identification must be improved and validated on development data before R2 (`s56-v1`) is frozen.

## 0. Correction of 2026-11-20 (before any annotation)

**Why.** Ori's quality review found two real problems in the first build. Evidence is in
`data/loc/review/quality/` (local) and `data/loc/review/lexicon_priority.csv`.
1. **False form matches.**
   - A generator bug dropped the final נ in every form (ספן gave יספו, שכן gave ישכם).
   - Unvocalized causatives looked like simple forms (ויקמו).
   - Homograph roots slipped in (ישם from שמם).
   - Of 40 form-only Mikra matches read, only 25 were the verb.
2. **Homographs filled the B strata.**
   - Mikra: 43 of 121 B items (זמר "sing", כפר "atone", גמל "repay", ספן = יסף).
   - Modern: 24 of 87 B items (טען "claim", ציפה "expect").

**What changed.**

| Part | Change |
|---|---|
| Generator `morph-v2` | Final nun assimilates only in the perfect before a consonant ending (נתתי). I-nun/I-yod deletion only where Hebrew makes it. The hollow ו/י swap only in the causative. Only existing prefix/ending pairs in the imperfect. No rare ן suffix |
| Forms rule `forms-v2` | Evidence for another verb now ignores Dicta's junk lemmas (`##` fragments, copies of the word itself). A form is **clean** at ≤ 10% foreign lemmas, **ambiguous** at 10–50% (kept, with a `~` partner naming the other verb), **dropped** above 50%. A causative or intensive form that is also a simple form of the same root gets the partner `~<root>.S`. Dicta-attested forms outside the root's paradigm need stronger support (≥ 5 tokens and ≥ 50% of the word), which removes cases like ימליכו |
| Identification | A `~` partner is added only when the entry matched by form alone. Dicta's own lemma is never overruled. Still one row per token, one combined key |
| Sense screen (pre-registered) | Every key that makes up ≥ 5% of its stratum, in Mikra or Modern, was read on 15 instances outside the sample. It is flagged when ≤ 3 are the lexicon verb (a different lexeme, not an abstract use). **Flagged:** כפר.S, גמל.S, שוך.S (Mikra), and from the review זמר.S and טען.S. Their lemma becomes a shared lemma, or their forms get a partner: they stay in, as `~` keys. Summary: `data/loc/review/quality/sense_screen_summary.csv` |
| Sampler `gold-r1b` | New **HOMOGRAPH** stratum for every key with a `~` member. It gets its population share, capped at 10% of the core, with at most 4 items per key, so homographs never fill A, A/B or B. Weights: `pi` (design inclusion probability), `weight_ht` = 1/pi, and the calibrated `weight`, which sums exactly to each stratum's population |
| Redraw (authorized by Ori) | One redraw before any annotation. `sample_id` is now `<corpus>-r1b-…`. The manifest records `supersedes` (the r1 rehearsal ids, never annotated) and `redraw_reason`. A `FROZEN_SAMPLES` guard is filled at release, so the split can never change again |

**Generated forms, measured once against BHSA** (`hebloc loc lexicon bhsa-eval`, mapping
`config/reference/locative_bhsa.yaml`). BHSA lexeme plus stem is the truth. It is never used to choose lemmas, forms
or flags. Mikra: 12,956 true tokens in 23,095 aligned verses.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1: the first build (forms-v1) | 0.757 | 0.734 | 0.745 | 0.757 / 0.730 / 0.743 | 78 |
| V2-L: forms-v2, Dicta-attested forms only | 0.767 | 0.635 | 0.695 | 0.775 / 0.624 / 0.692 | 279 |
| V2: forms-v2 with generated forms for every entry | 0.574 | 0.845 | 0.684 | **0.797 / 0.719 / 0.756** | 7,354 |
| **R1 as built: the pre-registered fallback** | 0.713 | 0.657 | 0.684 | 0.777 / 0.624 / 0.692 | 1,528 |

- **The pre-registered rule** kept generated forms only if (a) their clean matches reach precision ≥ 0.80 **and**
  (b) F1(V2) ≥ F1(V2-L).
  - (a) passed: 0.836.
  - (b) failed: 0.684 < 0.695.
  - So **the fallback applies**: generated forms only for entries with no Dicta lemma of their own, always marked
    `~form`.
- **The rule's F1 counted `~` tokens as identifications.** Those tokens are deliberately flagged as possibly another
  verb, and never feed the class analysis.
- **On the tokens that do feed the class analysis (clean keys), V2 is best on precision, recall and F1.**
  - **Ori kept the pre-registered fallback** (2026-11-20).
  - V2's generated-form precision on clean keys (0.836) is recorded as a lead for Phase C. It is **not** adopted

- **The miss causes are recorded per configuration** in `data/loc/review/quality/bhsa/*.json`. Examples: Dicta POS

### 0.1 Release conditions and known limits of identification (Ori, 2026-11-20)

**R1 is released as a provisional infrastructure milestone.** Its identification is usable for drawing and
annotating the gold. It is **not** a validated final system.

**The numbers below are measured once on Mikra against BHSA** (same 23,095 verses, same 12,956 true tokens, same
mapping and scoring for every configuration, so they are directly comparable). They are recorded as found.

**What the release requires.**
1. **The frozen gold samples never change** (§3). Instances recovered later by better identification enter the
   gold only through the **supplemental sampling procedure** (§3).
2. **Modern ישב.I stays in its stratum** (B, 8 items) **for human sense annotation.** The guideline (§4.15) tells

   split is reported from the gold labels.
3. **Identification must be addressed and validated before R2 (`s56-v1`) is frozen** (Phase C).

   - **Validation** is read only for the pre-registered choice between variants. **Test** is read once, at the

   - **No retuning against BHSA:** its single pre-registered use is done. A later BHSA figure may be reported

   - Items for Phase C: lemma-only template errors (limit 3, 4); blank-lemma real verbs (limit 1, 5); V2's

   - The gold sample is drawn from R1's identified tokens, so it measures precision and frames, **not recall**.
     Recall is measured on Mikra by BHSA (above); for the other corpora it is reported as a limit.

- Mikra is typed now, at the ≥ 2 cut.
- The Hazal, Medieval and Haskala lists are produced **only after Efrat's Modern types are merged**. The

**Readers:** Ori (review); after release, Efrat and any stage-2 annotator.

### 1.3 How a verb is found in Dicta's output (identification)

A token counts as an occurrence of an entry when **Dicta tags it VERB** and **either** of these holds:
1. **Its lemma is one of the entry's `dicta_lemmas`.** These are the spellings Dicta actually uses for that verb.
   They were checked against Dicta's verb inventory of all five corpora: 453 variants, plus 6 shared lemmas. They include
   the participle or inflected strings Dicta often uses as lemmas: מביא, זורק, מטמא, לקחתי, וישם…
2. **Its host form (the word without ו/ה/ב… prefixes) is in the entry's generated form list**
   (`config/locative_verb_forms.yaml`, generated, never hand-edited).

**Why the forms are needed.** On Mikra, Dicta is often wrong or blank on exactly these rare verbs:
- 14.7% of Mikra verb tokens have no lemma;
- for about 40 entries Dicta has **no lemma of their own in any corpus**, e.g. הגלה, גילח, כיבס, המטיר, הפשיט,
  חיסר.

**How the forms are built.**
1. Every unvocalized form of the root in that template is generated: all persons, both spellings, weak roots, object
   suffixes (`hebloc/loc/morph.py`). This is linguistic knowledge, **never BHSA**, because BHSA is the yardstick for
   measuring identification in Phase C.
2. A form is kept only if Dicta's data allows it (`forms-v2`, §0):
   - it occurs as a VERB host in the corpora;
   - ≤ 10% of its tokens carry another real verb's lemma: **clean**;
   - 10–50%: **ambiguous**, kept with a `~` partner;
   - more: **dropped**.

   Blank lemmas, `##` fragments, copies of the word itself and rare odd strings are neutral.
3. **Generated forms are used only for entries with no Dicta lemma of their own, and are always marked `~form`.**
   This is the pre-registered fallback after the BHSA measurement (§0). Every other entry uses only Dicta-attested
   forms.
4. Result: 6,717 forms, of which 2,322 are ambiguous; 991 candidate forms dropped. (The first build had 12,155.)

**What is deliberately excluded.** Variants that are mostly another word: פונה "turning", חוטא "sinner", קודש
"holiness", חוסר "lack", יושב "sitting". Each exclusion is listed in the entry, with its reason.

### 1.4 The ambiguity rule: one token = one instance, nothing forced, nothing counted twice

- **When several entries match**, the token gets one **combined key**, e.g. `שלח.I|שלח.S`. Dicta never writes
  שילח in Mikra, so Simple שָׁלַח (A) and Intensive שִׁלַּח (A/B) cannot be told apart.
  - The token is never given to one of them by default, and never counted under each.
  - Its class is computed like any other. It is compared with the paper only when all members share one class
    (e.g. קבץ.S and קיבץ.I are both A); otherwise it is labelled *not comparable*.
- **When the lemma is also a non-lexicon verb's lemma**, the key gets a `~` member: ציפה "overlay" vs ציפה
  "expect" gives `צפה.I|~ציפה`.
- **On Mikra** (Phase C), BHSA's lexeme and stem can split a combined key, as a sensitivity check only.
- **When a form is shared with another verb** (an ambiguous form, §0), an entry matched by form alone gets that
  verb as a `~` partner, e.g. `קום.C|~קום.S` for ויקמו.
- **Measured on Mikra (R1 as built):**
  - 12,045 identified tokens (the first build had 12,704).
  - Match method: lemma only 3,165; form only 1,822; both 7,058.
  - Key kind: single 9,648; combined 846; with a `~` member 1,551.
  - BHSA confirms the combined keys are genuine: 98% of them contain the true verb.

### 1.5 What `hebloc loc lexicon check` verifies

**Always, from the config alone** (this runs on Efrat's computer):
- the schema and values;
- unique ids and unique (root, template);
- the counts against the paper, where only the documented discrepancies are allowed;
- every lemma shared by two entries is flagged;
- the forms file matches the lexicon.

**With `--data`, it also reports:**
- the frequency of every lemma variant per corpus (zero-frequency variants: none);
- the near-miss lemmas, i.e. the same root in another template. These are left out on purpose: for example
  intransitive עלה is not the entry העלה;
- whether each referenced paper verse in Mikra contains the entry.

**Paper references found: 125/219** (142 in the first build, which matched more generated forms). The misses fall into
three groups:
1. **Expected:** the reference is to the base or intransitive verb, not the entry. Examples: Table 4's mereological
   column (אכל, Proverbs 12:11), fn 16's intransitive uses, Table 2's change-of-state column.
2. **Dicta tags the verb as a noun or proper noun:** e.g. שמתיה, משחתיו, ספן.
3. **Dicta gives another real verb's lemma:** e.g. יבזו → ביזה, יפח → הפיח, אשטף → ליטף, ומחסר → הורס.

The precision rule refuses these forms, because they are genuinely ambiguous: יבזו can be בזז or בזה. Phase C
measures this lost recall against BHSA on all of Mikra. Any fix would then be a recorded decision, never a silent
change.

**Ori's review sheets** are in `data/loc/review/`:
- `lexicon_review.csv`: one row per entry, with its class, list, flags, lemma variants and token counts, forms,
  paper references found, the lemmas that block its forms, idioms and MH prediction;
- `lexicon_review_near_misses.csv`;
- `lexicon_review_paper_refs.csv`.

## 2. The annotation guideline (`annotation/S56_GUIDELINE.md`, version `S56-v1-g1`)

**Who annotates:**
- Ori: Mikra;
- Efrat: Modern;
- both: the agreement items;
- whoever runs Hazal, Medieval or Haskala in stage 2.

**The procedure.** It follows 5.8, step by step:
1. Is it this verb?
2. Find the arguments in the sentence itself.
3. Is the use locative?
4. Locatum and Location.
5. The frame follows from the role of the direct object.

The preposition is never used alone to decide the frame, because the paper claims it is the same in both frames.

**Special cases**, each with the paper's own example:
- passives;
- an elided object;
- an elided Locatum ב- (Haggai 2:7);
- partitive מן (Leviticus 16:14);
- instrument ב- (Jeremiah 15:7);
- a recipient or possessor as the Location;
- על instead of ב;
- the Locatum as subject;
- change of state;
- idioms;
- clausal complements;
- double accusatives.

The guideline also gives worked BH and MH examples.

**Human only.** No AI pre-labelling. The splits are annotated in order: dev, then validation, then test.

## 3. The gold sampler (`hebloc loc gold sample`)

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Population** | Every lexicon-identified VERB token of the corpus, in the full view. Identification only, so the gold also measures how much the extractor misses |
| **Strata** | Each token is in exactly one stratum. **HOMOGRAPH** (gold-r1b): any key with a `~` member, so possibly another verb. **A**, **A/B** or **B**: all members are core entries of that class. **MIXED**: core members of different classes, e.g. `שלח.I\|שלח.S`. **MH**: §8 supplement only (Modern). **NONPAPER** (Modern, for §6): a non-lexicon verb with a direct object **and** a ב/מן-family PP. Tokens of `paper_other` entries only are counted but not sampled |
| **Allocation** | Mikra 400; Modern 270 core + 30 MH + 100 NONPAPER; Hazal, Medieval and Haskala 150 each (test only). MIXED gets its share of the population; HOMOGRAPH its share, capped at 10% of the core; **A, A/B and B get equal thirds of the rest** (Ori's choice) |
| **Cap** | At most **8 items per verb key** (4 in HOMOGRAPH, 2 per lemma in NONPAPER), so that נתן, שלח and לקח do not fill the sample |
| **Weights** | `pi` = the design inclusion probability; `weight_ht` = 1/pi; `weight` = calibrated: each sampled key stands for its own population, scaled for the keys not drawn, so the weights of a stratum sum **exactly** to its population (checked in the manifest) |
| **Order** | A fixed hash: sha256(`S56-v1:<corpus>:gold:` + `sent_id\|piece_idx\|word_idx`). No randomness, no seed to lose |
| **Split** | By rank within each stratum: 1 dev : 1 validation : 2 test |
| **Agreement** | 60 items (15 dev + 15 validation + 30 test), chosen by a second hash. Efrat labels Ori's Mikra 60; Ori labels Efrat's Modern 60 |
| **Shortfall** | Recorded, never filled with substitutes |
| **Same sample everywhere** | `sample_id` depends on the corpus, the lexicon hash and the sampler version, not on the data source. **Repo and package give byte-identical samples and sheets** (tested on Mikra and on Modern) |

**Results:**

| Corpus | sample_id | A | A/B | B | HOMOGRAPH | MIXED | MH | NONPAPER | Splits (dev / val / test) | Shortfall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mikra | `mikra-r1b-1cd4ee9802` | 111 | 111 | 98 | 40 | 27 | — | — | 98 / 98 / 191 | B 13 |
| Modern | `modern-r1b-5fbb5e22e7` | 79 | 80 | 80 | 27 | 4 | 30 | 100 | 101 / 101 / 198 | none |

The r1 rehearsal samples (`mikra-r1-6bf0bae6b4`, `modern-r1-d5ae20ec4e`) are superseded. They were never annotated,
and are archived in `data/loc/archive/r1-v1/`.

**Mikra B shortfall (13).** Without generated forms for entries that have a Dicta lemma, only 98 B tokens are
available at the cap of 8 per key. The shortfall is recorded, never filled.

**Population** (Mikra / Modern): A 7,512 / 1,302,541; A/B 1,168 / 281,880; B 395 / 21,655; HOMOGRAPH 1,177 / 302,123; MIXED 749 / 25,734; MH 120 / 112,473; paper_other only 924 / 68,802; NONPAPER — / 855,993. Modern's B population was 154,179 before the correction: 82% of it was טען "claim" and ציפה "expect", which are now in HOMOGRAPH.

**The B strata are now free of the homographs.**
- Mikra: 0 of 98 B items from the suspect keys (זמר, כפר, גמל, ספן, צפה~, דשן, קרה), against 43 of 121 before.
- Modern: 0 of 80 B items (טען, צפה, כפר), against 24 of 87 before. **ישב.I (8 items) is kept for human sense
  annotation** (§0.1; guideline §4.15): its Modern uses are often "resolve (a dispute)", which the annotator labels
  non-locative. It is never excluded automatically.

**Weights.** In both corpora, the calibrated weights sum to each stratum's population (ratio 1.0 everywhere, in the manifest). The sampled keys cover 85–100% of each class stratum's population.

**Repo = package.** Byte-identical `sample.parquet`, `sample.csv`, `population.parquet` and all four sheets, for both corpora.

**Run time:** Mikra 1 s; Modern about 9 minutes (repo 9.5, package 8.6) and about 3.5 GB of memory.

**Test independence.**
- The split is fixed at the draw, recorded with the lexicon hash.
- `gold check` writes each split to its own text-free file.
- Rule development (R2) reads only the paper examples and dev; validation is read only for the pre-registered
  choice of variant; test is read once, with an explicit `--final-test`.
- A later change to the lexicon never redraws the sample.

**Frozen at the release (D-057).** `FROZEN_SAMPLES` in `gold.py` holds each sample's id, `items_sha256` (the
fingerprint of every item's id, instance, stratum, split and agreement flag) and the `sample.parquet` sha256.
- `gold sample` refuses a different id, and refuses the same id with different items. Nothing is written then.
- The manifest records `frozen_check` (`items_match`, `sample_file_matches`).
- Efrat's draw from the package must give the same ids and `items_match: true`. Anything else is a STOP.

**Supplemental sampling procedure (for instances recovered later).** If Phase C makes identification find tokens
that R1 did not, the frozen sample is **never** redrawn or edited. Instead:
1. The new tokens (identified by the new version, not by R1) form a separate population, recorded with both
   lexicon/forms versions.
2. A **supplemental sample** is drawn from that population only, with the same strata, caps, hash order and
   1:1:2 split, a new salt, and its own id `<corpus>-s1-<hash>`. Its manifest names the frozen sample it supplements.
3. Its weights refer to the supplemental population only. Estimates combine the frozen sample and the supplement
   as two separate strata of one design.
4. The test items of the supplement are read only at the final test, like the frozen test.
5. Ori approves each supplement, with a decision entry, before it is drawn.

**Outputs** are written to `data/loc/gold/<corpus>/<sample_id>/`:
- `sample.parquet` / `sample.csv` (ids only), `population.parquet`, `manifest.json`;
- `sheets/` **(local only: they quote corpus text, D-042).**

**The text-free gold file (I4)** is written by
`hebloc loc gold check <sheet> --sample <folder> --annotator <name> --out <folder>`.

## 4. The frequent-argument-noun lists (`hebloc loc nouns list`)

**What is listed.** The NOUN and PROPN lemmas that head a direct object, a passive subject or a PP of the lexicon
verbs. In Modern, also those of every verb with a full frame, for §6. Each is listed with its counts as DO, as
ב-PP, as מן-PP and as other PP, and with its verbs.

**How they are typed.**
- Claude Code may propose a type (`proposed_by: claude_code`); the human accepts or corrects every row
  (`reviewed_by`).
- `hebloc loc nouns merge` refuses rows without a human reviewer, and writes `config/noun_types.yaml` (Ori only).

## 6. The annotation workload (to decide before any annotation starts)

**Who does what (as designed in S56-v1 5.8 and §4 above):**

| Person | Task | Size | Time (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ori | Review the lexicon (`lexicon_review.csv`) | 210 entries | 2–4 h |
| Ori | Mikra gold: dev, then validation, then test | 400 items (+ the paper's examples, already labelled by the paper) | 7–20 h at 1–3 min/item |
| Ori | Modern agreement items (Efrat's sample) | 60 items | 1–3 h |
| Ori | Noun types: Mikra now; the new nouns of Hazal, Medieval, Haskala after Efrat's Modern types (option A) | 991 + ≈ 113 rows | 2–3 h at 5–10 s/row |
| Efrat | Modern gold | 400 items | 7–20 h |
| Efrat | Mikra agreement items | 60 items | 1–3 h |
| Efrat | Modern noun types (Claude Code proposes, Efrat decides) | 1,763 rows | 3–5 h |
| Stage 2 (later) | Hazal, Medieval, Haskala test gold | 150 each | about 5–8 h each |

The time per item is an **assumption**. Time the first 20 items; the estimate is then corrected.

**Why these sizes:**
- **Test, 200 per corpus.** The test is the one number the report gives for the automatic frame decision. With
  n = 200, the Wilson 95% confidence interval of an accuracy is about ±5.5 points at 80% and ±6.9 points at 50%.
  With n = 100 it would be ±7.8 and ±9.8 points.
- **Validation, 100.** It is used only to choose between R0, R1 and R2 (macro-F1, McNemar). With fewer items, a
  real difference would rarely be significant, and the simpler variant would win by default.
- **Dev, 100 plus the paper's examples.** These are what the rules are developed against, without touching
  validation or test.
- **Agreement, 60 + 60.** This gives a κ with a 95% interval of about ±0.12. That shows whether the guideline is
  reproducible, and whether one annotator per corpus is enough.
- **Equal thirds per class.** Without it, B items would be about 6% of a random Mikra sample (759 of 11,914 core
  tokens). Frame B, which the claim needs, would then be measured on about 20 items.

**Reduction options, and what each costs:**

| Option | Saves | Cost to research quality |
|---|---|---|
| a. Mikra noun cut at ≥ 3 instead of ≥ 2 | about 320 rows (720 instead of 1,042; coverage 87.4% instead of 92.8%) | Small: the untyped rare nouns are handled by the induced score (R1) or stay UNDETERMINED |
| b. Historical noun coverage at 80% instead of 85% | about 360 rows | Small to moderate: more UNDETERMINED in stage 2 |
| c. Agreement 60 → 40 per corpus | 20 items per person | κ interval about ±0.15: still informative |
| d. Mikra gold 400 → 300 (100 test) | 100 items | **Large:** the test interval widens to ±8–10 points, the main validation number of §5 |
| e. Have Claude Code propose the gold | — | **Not allowed** (D-054): the gold would stop being an independent yardstick |

**Recommendation:** keep the gold sizes. If time is short, take (a) and possibly (c), which together save about
6–8 hours across both people with little loss.

## 8. Decision D-057: R1 implementation clarifications (accepted by Ori 2026-11-20, release `s56-r1`)

These choices are left open by S56-v1. None of them changes the methodology.

1. **Identification requires upos VERB, for lemma matches too.** Otherwise the noun שם "name" would match the verb
   שם "put". Dicta's POS errors on Mikra (e.g. שמתיה tagged PROPN) are therefore a measured loss of recall.
2. **`dicta_lemmas`** are the spellings Dicta actually outputs for the verb, checked against the inventory of all
   five corpora. They include participle and inflected lemma strings (מביא, לקחתי…). They are never derived from
   BHSA. Excluded variants are listed in the entry, with their reason.
3. **`shared_lemmas`** (a lemma used for this verb and for a non-lexicon verb) produce a `~` member in the combined
   key.
4. **The fallback forms (`forms-v2`, `morph-v2`; corrected 2026-11-20, §0).**
   - Candidates: the hosts of the entry's own lemmas (≥ 2 tokens, ≥ 80% purity; outside the root's paradigm also
     ≥ 5 tokens and ≥ 50% of the word).
   - Generated paradigm forms only for entries with no Dicta lemma of their own, always `~form`. This is the
     pre-registered BHSA fallback, kept by Ori at the release.
   - A form is clean at ≤ 10% established foreign lemmas, ambiguous at 10–50% (with `~` partners), dropped above.
     Neutral lemmas: blank, `##` fragments, copies of the word.
   - A causative or intensive form that is also a simple form of the same root gets `~<root>.S`.
   - The counts are pooled over the five corpora, and the form file is generated and committed.
5. **The ambiguity rule** (Ori's condition): one token is one instance, with a combined key for any multiple match.
   Never forced, never counted twice. A class is compared with the paper only when all members share one class.
6. **אסר** is class A, with `paper_conflict`, compared under both readings. The 96/95 discrepancy is documented.
7. **The gold design (`gold-r1b`).**
   - Strata A, A/B, B, MIXED (core, by class), HOMOGRAPH (any `~` key), MH, and NONPAPER (Modern, with its sampling
     frame).
   - MIXED is proportional; HOMOGRAPH is proportional but capped at 10% of the core (4 per key); equal thirds for
     the rest; a cap of 8 per key (2 per lemma for NONPAPER).
   - Weights: `pi`, `weight_ht` and the calibrated `weight` (sums exactly to each stratum's population).
   - sha256 hash order; a 1:1:2 split by rank within each stratum.
   - 60 agreement items inside the sample (15/15/30); shortfalls recorded.
   - Hazal, Medieval and Haskala: 150, test only.
8. **Test independence** (Ori's condition): the split is fixed at the draw; each split is a separate text-free file;
   test labels are read only by an explicit `--final-test` scoring run; Claude Code does not open test sheets or
   labels during development.
9. **The sheets** live under `data/loc/gold/` (local, D-042); the text-free gold lives under `annotation/gold/` (in
   git). The gold file has an optional `member` column, for combined keys.
10. **The noun lists.**
    - Mikra: lemmas with ≥ 2 tokens.
    - Hazal, Medieval, Haskala, Modern: 85% coverage, at most 2,000 rows, incremental with `--exclude-typed`.
    - Every row must have a human reviewer.
11. **Paper references:** the paper's Hebrew parenthetical wins; table references are mapped by checking the verse.
    Every correction is noted in the entry.
12. **Releases and commands.**
    - R1 is the lightweight tag `s56-r1`.
    - Output commands refuse a dirty tree without `--dev`.
    - Added to I2: `loc gold check`, `loc nouns merge`, `loc lexicon inventory|forms|bhsa-eval`, `loc verify`.
13. **The one-time redraw** (authorized by Ori 2026-11-20, before any annotation).
    - The r1 rehearsal samples are superseded by `gold-r1b`; the manifest records `supersedes` and
      `redraw_reason`.
    - At release, `FROZEN_SAMPLES` records the approved ids, the item fingerprints and the file hashes. A
      different id, or the same id with different items, is refused.
14. **The sense screen** (pre-registered). Keys ≥ 5% of their stratum were read on 15 non-sample instances. A key is
    flagged when ≤ 3 are the lexicon verb **and** the rest are a different lexeme. Flagged: כפר.S, גמל.S, שוך.S,
    זמר.S, טען.S, which become shared lemmas or form partners, never deletions.
15. **Identification measured once against BHSA** (`config/reference/locative_bhsa.yaml`; stems: Simple =
    qal/nif, Intensive = piel/pual/hit…, Causative = hif/hof).
    - The pre-registered keep/fallback rule is applied as written.
    - The numbers are Phase C's identification baseline; no entry is tuned on them.
16. **Noun plan, option A.** The Mikra cut stays at ≥ 2. The historical lists are made only after Efrat's Modern
    noun types are merged.
17. **R1 is a provisional infrastructure milestone** (Ori, at the release). Its known identification limits are
    recorded in §0.1: recall 0.657 (clean 0.624), lemma-only precision 0.46, two wrong template assignments
    (Genesis 27:40, Jeremiah 30:17), paper references 125 of 219.
18. **Identification is addressed and validated before R2 is frozen**, on the dev split of the human gold. Validation
    is only for the pre-registered choice; test is read once, at the end. No retuning against BHSA.
19. **The supplemental sampling procedure** (§3) is the only way recovered instances enter the gold. The frozen
    samples never change.
20. **Sense review, not exclusion:** Modern ישב.I stays in its stratum and is labelled by the human annotator
    (guideline §4.15: locative, non-locative or `?`).
